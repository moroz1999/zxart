# Press articles

A press article is one article of a disk magazine. It always belongs to the
issue it was published in, and an issue's articles, in publishing order, are its
table of contents.

## What an article holds
- a short title, the one that appears in the table of contents
- a fuller title for the article's own page, falling back to the short one
- a short introduction summarising it
- the article text
- a link to where the text was taken from

The text is scene writing typed against a fixed-width grid, so it is shown
exactly as it was laid out.

## The original and the processed text
An article keeps the raw text it was typed or pasted from, exactly as submitted,
separately from the text the site shows. The shown text is produced from it
automatically: formatted, translated into the site's languages, and given its
introduction. The original is never overwritten by that processing, and it is
never replaced by the processed text — one is a source and the other is output.

An article that has not been processed into the visitor's language yet is shown
in its original form.

## What an article points at
An article links the authors, people, groups, software, graphics, music and
parties it mentions, so a work can show which magazines wrote about it.

## Reading an article
The article's page names its issue before its own title, so it is clear what is
being read. Beside the text sit the issue's cover, its year and its full table of
contents, with the current article marked, and a link to the issue itself. On a
narrow screen the table of contents is dropped rather than pushed above the
article.

The article is followed by links to the previous and next article of the same
issue, and by its discussion.

The page is built for reading: it opens plainly, and nothing on it competes with
the article text.
